# Master's Brief — GSIA Band-Algebra Audit

## The Primary Good

Clarity, for Globe & Atlas readers and professional peers who need to see the
difference between a band set, a formula, a named index, a proposed
specification, and a validated environmental method.

## The Stone to Be Removed

Delegate exhaustive enumeration, sign/direction canonicalization, exact
equation fingerprinting, source-snapshot checksums, and registry field
classification to code. Constrain that delegation so it never assigns
environmental usefulness, novelty, or validation from mathematical structure
alone.

## The Human Mark (The 20%)

Human judgment remains responsible for choosing the environmental target,
deciding which channels belong in a physically defensible search, naming hard
confusers, selecting established baselines, reviewing unexpected formulas, and
deciding whether evidence supports retain, revise, reclassify, or retire.

## The Non Finito Gaps

1. Do not automatically name or publish unmatched equations as new indices.
2. Do not close the performance gap with synthetic signatures; wait for labeled
   observations and held-out geography or time.
3. Do not search for a universal best formula. Leave target choice and domain of
   applicability explicit.

## The S.D.G. Filter (Soli Deo Gloria)

This work delivers clarity by making the limits of automated formula discovery
as inspectable as its possibilities.

## The Structural Weakness

The registry has formula and maturity governance, but it does not yet have a
shared labeled benchmark on which applicable formulas and their simpler
ablations can be compared.

## The Feynman Gap

The unresolved question is whether multi-band composites add stable
target-specific information beyond simpler established contrasts once
atmosphere, season, geography, spatial resolution, and confusers vary. Closing
that gap requires real observations and blocked validation, not more algebra.

## The Myth Under Test

The load-bearing assumption is that a bounded exhaustive search can identify
compact formulas that add transferable information beyond established indices.
The minimum viable test is one environmental target with independent labels,
hard negatives, one geographic or temporal holdout, and locked baseline
comparisons. Failure means no candidate consistently beats the simpler
baseline outside development data. If that occurs, the search becomes a
negative-result and failure-mode atlas rather than a new-index pipeline.

## The Misericord

The hidden work is formula provenance and equivalence: pinned source versions,
checksums, canonical role ordering, exact-equation matching, and preservation
of duplicate names that point to the same mathematics.
